# Agent 7 — The Resolution & Drafting Agent

_The communicator who turns the full plan into words your customer and your team can act on._

By the time a case reaches this stage, the hard work is done — the issue is understood, the fix is known, parts are planned, service is scheduled, and approvals are in place. What's left is the human moment: **telling the customer what happens next, in clear, confident language, and leaving your team with notes they can trust.** That's Agent 7. It drafts the customer update and the internal work notes so nothing gets lost between planning and delivery.

---

## What it does (in one line)

**It turns everything the AI team has figured out into ready-to-use customer messages and internal work notes — written in the right tone, aligned with policy, and easy for your people to review and send.**

---

## Scope — what this agent owns (and what it does not)

**Agent 7 owns communication drafting only.**

| In scope                                            | Out of scope                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Drafting customer-facing updates and replies        | Deciding what the fix should be                   |
| Writing internal work notes for the service team    | Checking inventory or ordering parts              |
| Summarizing the agreed plan in plain language       | Scheduling technicians                            |
| Matching tone to customer tier and situation        | Running approval checks                           |
| Including next steps, timelines, and what to expect | Sending messages without human review             |
| Referencing approved guidance where helpful         | Changing records or committing actions on its own |

Agent 7 **writes drafts**. It does not send emails, book appointments, or move inventory. Your team reviews the drafts, makes any edits, and sends when ready.

---

## Topic — what this agent is about

**Customer communication and internal documentation.**

Every resolved case needs two things: a message the customer understands, and notes the team can follow. Agent 7 answers the questions your best service rep would ask before hitting "send":

- What should we tell the customer — clearly and honestly?
- What tone fits this relationship — premium account, first-time caller, or repeat issue?
- What are the next steps, dates, and expectations we can safely share?
- What should the technician or case owner know before they pick up the work?
- Are there policy boundaries we need to respect in the wording (warranty, fees, escalation)?
- Does the message reflect everything already agreed — parts, visit window, and approvals?

Triage told you _how urgent_ the case is. Customer history told you _who you're dealing with_. Knowledge told you _what the fix looks like_. Parts and scheduling told you _when and how it gets done_. Guardrail made sure it was safe to proceed. Agent 7 tells everyone _what to say and what to document_.

## Why it matters

- **Customers get clarity, not confusion.** Instead of a vague "we're looking into it," they receive a clear update with real next steps.
- **Your team stops staring at a blank reply box.** The first draft is already written — informed by everything the earlier agents assembled.
- **Tone matches the relationship.** A strategic account gets a different voice than a standard support ticket — and Agent 7 adjusts automatically.
- **Internal handoffs stay clean.** Technicians and case owners open work notes that summarize the issue, the plan, and what was already promised — no hunting through six screens.
- **Policy stays in the message.** Warranty limits, approved wording, and escalation context are reflected in the draft — not added as an afterthought.
- **Consistency at scale.** Every case gets the same quality of communication, whether it's Tuesday morning or Sunday night.

---

## Steps it executes (the simple version)

Think of Agent 7 as a careful writer who has read the entire case file before putting pen to paper:

1. **Receives the fully prepared case** — picks up after Agents 1 through 6, with priority, customer context, approved guidance, parts plan, service schedule, and any required approvals already in place.
2. **Reads the full story** — understands what happened, what was decided, and what the customer should expect next.
3. **Chooses the right tone** — adjusts language for customer tier, urgency, and relationship (for example, premium SLA, repeat issue, or strategic account).
4. **Drafts the customer message** — writes a clear, friendly update explaining the situation, the plan, and what happens next.
5. **Drafts internal work notes** — captures what the service team needs: issue summary, agreed actions, parts and visit details, and any sensitivities.
6. **Aligns with policy** — respects warranty boundaries, approved guidance, and anything the guardrail step flagged.
7. **Highlights key dates and commitments** — includes visit windows, delivery estimates, and follow-up expectations the customer can rely on.
8. **Packages everything for review** — delivers ready-to-use drafts and passes the case to the final logging step.

The whole process runs automatically in seconds — no one has to rewrite the same update from scratch or worry they forgot to mention the appointment time.

## Built-in trust & safety

- **Drafts only — never auto-sends.** Agent 7 prepares messages for human review. Nothing reaches the customer without your team's sign-off.
- **Built from approved facts.** Every draft draws on what the earlier agents already verified — not invented details or optimistic promises.
- **Policy-respecting language.** Warranty limits, fee boundaries, and escalation context are reflected in the wording.
- **Tone without overpromising.** The message explains the plan honestly — including realistic timelines, not guaranteed dates that operations cannot keep.
- **Privacy aware.** Customer messages use appropriate business language — not unnecessary internal jargon or sensitive internal notes.
- **A specialist, not a decision-maker.** Agent 7 writes what should be said. It does not reschedule, reorder parts, approve spend, or close the case — those actions belong to your people and your systems.

In short: **Agent 7 closes the loop between "we know what to do" and "everyone knows what to say."**
